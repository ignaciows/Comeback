# What the app can work out on its own

The goal is that Comeback asks as little as possible and notices as much as
possible. Every question in an app is a chance to be answered wrong, forgotten,
or abandoned — and most of what a training app asks for is already implied by
what the user does inside it.

This is the audit of that: what is derivable from ordinary use, what each
signal is worth, and what is deliberately left alone.

Everything below lives in `src/domain/inference/` and is pure — no React, no
storage, no network. It runs on data the user generated by training.

---

## Derivable today, and built

| Signal | Derived from | What it changes | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time of day you train** | `startedAt` on completed sessions | When the reminder fires | 3 sessions |
| **Days of the week you train** | Session dates | The planned schedule | 3 sessions |
| **How long a session takes you** | Start to end timestamps | Session estimates, how much work is laid out | 3 sessions |
| **How long you rest** | Gaps between set timestamps | The rest timer default | 3 sessions |
| **Your strongest weekday** | Working sets per session, by weekday | Where the hardest session goes | 8 sessions, 2 per weekday |
| **Whether you finish sessions** | Completed sets ÷ laid-out sets | How much gets laid out | 3 sessions |
| **Exercises you avoid** | Repeated substitutions and untouched exercises | Dropped from the routine | 2 occurrences |
| **Muscles you actually train** | Sets per muscle over 28 days | The volume chart, focus suggestions | any history |
| **Whether you are drifting away** | Gap since last session vs *your own* usual gap | The nudge on Today | 3 sessions |
| **How often you weigh in** | Dates of body measurements | How often the app asks | 3 measurements |

### Two rules the whole layer obeys

**Nothing is claimed from too little data.** Three sessions is not a habit.
Each function returns `null` rather than a shaky guess, and every observation
carries its sample size so the screen can say "from your last 9 sessions"
instead of asserting a fact.

**A best day has to actually be better.** `inferBestWeekday` returns nothing
unless the leading day beats the average of the rest by 15%. Otherwise it would
crown a winner from noise every single week.

### Detail worth knowing

- **Training time is averaged around the clock, not along a number line.**
  Someone who trains at 23:00 and 01:00 trains around midnight, not around
  noon. The circular mean also yields a spread, and a start time that swings by
  more than two hours produces no reminder at all — that is not a habit to
  nudge against.
- **Drop-off is judged against your own rhythm.** Four days off is nothing for
  someone who trains weekly and a real gap for someone who trains every second
  day. A fixed "we miss you at 7 days" is wrong for almost everybody.
- **A set counts fully for the muscle the exercise is built around and half for
  the ones assisting.** Bench press is chest work that also loads triceps.
  Counting it as full triceps volume inflates every pushing muscle and makes
  the volume chart lie.

## From what the app deduces to what it does

`deriveProposals` turns observations into changes, split by who gets to decide:

- **`auto` — the app corrects its own guess.** Rest length and session length
  were assumptions on day one. Once there is measured evidence, keeping the
  assumption is the strange choice. Applied without asking.
- **`ask` — the change alters what the user committed to.** Training days,
  weekly frequency, dropping an exercise. Offered with the evidence attached,
  never applied silently.

Nothing in this layer touches a goal, a target weight or a calorie number. Those
are decisions, not observations.

## Deliberately not built

**App-open times.** Cheap to record, but it answers a question nothing acts on:
the reminder time comes from when sessions *start*, not when the app is opened.
Tracking it would be data collected because it was possible.

**Screen time and phone pickups.** iOS does not expose Screen Time to third-party
apps at all. There is no honest version of this feature.

**Location-based gym detection.** Technically possible — the gym's coordinates
are already known from the search. It needs background location permission,
which is a large ask for the benefit of not tapping "Start", and it drains
battery. Revisit only if the reminder proves not to be enough.

**Inferring diet from weight change.** Weight change over time does imply
average intake, but only with a stable and known training load, and the error
bars are wider than the effect being measured. The app would be inventing
precision.

**Per-exercise fatigue modelling.** Real, but it needs RIR logged consistently
on most sets. Most users do not. Once the logging data supports it, it goes
here.

## What a wearable adds

The signals above need no hardware — they come from the phone the user is
already tapping. Apple Health, when connected, replaces estimates with
measurements: sleep from the watch instead of a typed number, resting heart
rate and HRV for the recovery half of Momentum, real workout duration and
heart rate instead of timestamps between sets.

The interfaces are the same either way (`src/services/health/`), so nothing
downstream changes when a watch appears. See `docs/health-integration.md`.

## Where the code is

```
src/domain/inference/observations.ts   what can be measured
src/domain/inference/proposals.ts      what that justifies changing
src/domain/training/volume.ts          weekly sets per muscle, and shifting them
tests/inference.test.ts                including the cases that must stay silent
```

The tests that matter most are the negative ones: that two sessions produce no
claim, that an identical week produces no "best day", that a single swap is not
a preference, and that a scattered training time produces no reminder. An app
that deduces is only trustworthy if it also knows when it cannot.
